# Scalar LZ4 physical multitasking pressure pilot

Six randomized rounds compared the small-table scalar candidate (`lz4-small`, 4 KiB hash table), its private 16 KiB-table matched control (`lz4-control`), normal LZ4 and zstd in the same separate namespaced research ZRAM module, on the physical Intel N100 running `7.2.11-n100-gzram-btf`. The normal LZ4 and zstd backend source files in that module match the running kernel's backend source byte for byte, but the driver itself is a separate coexistence prototype, not the active stock ZRAM device. The existing zstd and hibernation swap stayed active. Each trial used a temporary 192 MiB higher-priority swap device and the same bounded cgroup workload as the earlier generated-browser and compiler pilot: a 128 MiB synthetic working set, four compiler syntax checks, and eight generated local headless-browser launches in pairs. All checks passed. The test device was swapped off, reset and removed; the research module was unloaded, and the original swap configuration was verified unchanged. Temporary swap priority was global, so unrelated processes could contribute to its allocation and energy.

The [table](tables.md) gives absolute medians and medians of same-round paired percentage changes. Against its matched control, small-table median workload was 7.628 versus 7.573 s (+0.4% paired time); median browser launch was 1.821 versus 1.813 s (+0.4%); and package energy was 48.70 versus 48.53 J (-1.3% paired). Median sampled peak device allocation was 73.8 versus 71.4 MiB. The pooled absolute medians and paired percentage medians are different statistics. Standard LZ4 in the same module had median 7.611 s and zstd 7.883 s; zstd's sampled peak allocation was 63.2 MiB. These results do not demonstrate a meaningful end-to-end small-table improvement. Six short trials per codec are insufficient to prove equivalence or a stable regression, and sampled allocations are not exact workload-only totals.

Package RAPL includes service setup, monitoring and unrelated CPU-package activity, not whole-system energy or battery life. The separate 10 ms scheduler probe is not an interactive GUI latency measurement. The generated headless workload is not representative user browsing; no hardware cycles, long-term stability, actual app interactivity, or production configuration comparison was established. Raw results, random order, code, cgroup samples, fault counts and scheduler samples are retained. This pilot does not justify promoting the candidate or changing stock zstd. The user accepted manual reset if a hang occurred; independent automatic hard-hang recovery remains unverified.
