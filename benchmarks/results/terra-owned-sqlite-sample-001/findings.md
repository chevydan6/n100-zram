# Owned synthetic SQLite resident-page samples

Two fresh, separate processes populated and queried an in-memory SQLite database with 12,000 deterministic generated documents, then ran for about 25 seconds each. The sampler read only present pages of the direct child it launched, using a sanitized environment and PID/parent/name checks. It retained aggregate codec outcomes and source hashes, not addresses, page contents, database contents, or unrelated process data. Each trial sampled a reservoir of at most 128 anonymous private and 128 file-executable pages, on the physical Intel N100 with Linux 7.2.11-200.fc44.x86_64. Source, binaries and environment are identified in the JSON artifacts. The child and sampler ran at nice 10 under the exclusive native-test lock; stock zstd ZRAM and swap remained active and untouched.

In trial 1, 128 selected anonymous pages yielded 211,051 capped logical bytes for AVX2 GHY versus 204,147 for 4 KiB-table scalar LZ4 (+3.38%). Ten pages used GZRAM mode; all ten were larger than their LZ4 controls (15,800 versus 9,840 bytes). In trial 2, 128 anonymous pages yielded 221,743 versus 206,602 bytes (+7.33%); 15 GZRAM-mode pages were all larger (34,552 versus 20,315). The other pages used hybrid LZ4 framing and incurred eight bytes of framing per page. Trial 1 had 1,347 eligible anonymous reads; trial 2 had 1,336. File-executable aggregates are preserved but such pages can be reclaimed without swap and must not be treated as swap traffic.

The application and documents are synthetic; these are small, unweighted resident-page reservoirs, not the frequency of actual reclaim pages, and snapshots may repeat the same logical content between trials. Unavailable page reads and nonresident pages are excluded and counts are recorded. Logical payload is capped at 4 KiB, not allocator-rounded ZRAM memory. The sample does not measure codec time, package energy, latency, workload speed or real-world memory pressure. Each selected page round-tripped through the corresponding userspace decoder; prior independent decoder and sanitizer tests apply to the already-validated parent library, not to a new kernel path.

The results reinforce the need to avoid unnecessary GZRAM work on application pages, but do not establish a new classifier or overall compressor winner. Broader workloads and paired CPU/energy measurements are needed before adopting any selection change. Experimental native kernel testing remains gated on verified recovery.
