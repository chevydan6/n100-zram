# GCC LTO confirmation with package energy

Two fresh randomized nine-round userspace sessions compared the LTO and matched non-LTO Zstandard 1.5.7 builds from the [initial screen](../terra-zstd-n100-lto-001/findings.md). Each used 26 corpora, 128 pages per corpus and 1,024 calls per timing batch; 3,328 checked pages per session encoded identically and passed cross-library decoding, truncation, undersized-output and canary checks. Package RAPL was sampled around compression and decompression batches. It includes Python/ctypes overhead and other CPU-package activity; these short intervals do not measure whole-system or battery energy. Hardware cycles, full-codec sanitizers and native kernel performance were not measured. The initial session ran unprivileged; the confirmation sessions ran as root to read package energy, so they are separate operating conditions.

The [table](tables.md) reports medians of same-round percentage changes by session, with all corpora and decompression regressions. The initial bash compression change was -2.6%, but the two confirmation sessions were +3.8% and +1.9%; initial git was -5.3%, followed by +2.0% and +0.5%. Mixed pages were -7.0%, then +0.8% and -0.9%. Compression energy changes were similarly variable and can reflect background package activity and counter granularity. The earlier apparent broad gain did not repeat, so there is no credible overall LTO or energy improvement on this evidence. This does not prove LTO cannot help a differently built kernel; kernel LTO would require an appropriate supported toolchain/configuration, correctness and physical ZRAM evaluation. No boot settings, active zstd swap or kernel were changed.
