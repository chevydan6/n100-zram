# Zero-prefix fallback experiments: provisional checkpoint

Two additive GHY1 encoder policies test whether a short, zero-prefixed page with a recurring eight-byte key should fall back to LZ4 after GZRAM produces a relatively large frame. One requires one recurrence; the other requires two. They retain independent GHY1 page decoding and its wire format, but intentionally change some encoder selections and compressed bytes. Neither is recommended for routine use.

Normal and ASan/UBSan suites passed for the two-recurrence policy, including 177,312 cross-round trips, 118,160 guarded-capacity cases, 107,520 sparse-mask cases, 120,000 malformed or mutated inputs and 4,000 four-thread round trips per suite. The targeted tests exercise independently decoded frames, alignments and guarded capacities. The rebuilt parent and one-recurrence libraries have identical `.text` sections to their previously tested counterparts; whole-library hashes differ. See `validation.json` and the logs for scope and counts.

In targeted adversarial synthetic fixtures, the one-recurrence policy changed selection 995 times: 147 smaller, 842 larger, six equal. The stricter policy changed selection 480 times: 60 smaller and 420 larger. These are fixture/parameter combinations, not a frequency estimate for real applications. They show that matching recurrence alone does not establish a better compression choice.

In sample 005, taken from three owned compiler children, the one-recurrence candidate changed seven of 159 sampled non-same-word anonymous pages; all seven were smaller. Total capped logical payload was 205,142 bytes for the parent, 198,909 for the candidate, and 152,619 for scalar LZ4. This is an unweighted, small resident-page sample, not the distribution of ZRAM swap traffic or allocator consumption. In independent sample 006, a hypothetical two-recurrence probe fired five times, each smaller (6,436 to 2,674 bytes for those five). No speed or energy measurement accompanies either sample. File-backed executable pages are not necessarily swapped and these measurements do not establish real-world frequency.

The synthetic corpus diagnostic retains all fixtures, including cases specifically designed to trigger false positives. The first diagnostic attempt failed due to a stale expected corpus count; its failure is a harness error, not a codec result. The corrected diagnostic is retained in `corpus-diagnostic.json`. More realistic sampled pages and matched timing remain in progress. Kernel and ZRAM results, allocation rounding and system energy are unproven. The installed GRUB entries were not visible during the user's recovery check, so physical experimental kernel testing remains gated on verified recovery.

A separate sample 007 tested both actual encoders on the same pages: of 151 non-same-word anonymous pages both changed five selections, all smaller; parent 172,330 and both candidates 167,586 capped logical bytes. No sampled executable pages changed. These pages are sampled resident compiler memory, and the same limitations above apply. The 36-corpus benchmark passed normal and sanitizer round-trip checks, and all 34 previously existing corpus hashes matched; the smoke run is not used for a performance claim. See `benchmark-validation.json`.
