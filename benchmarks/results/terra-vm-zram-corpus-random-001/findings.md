# Shuffled guest ZRAM allocator observations

Three fresh diskless, networkless exact-kernel KVM guests repeated the [per-corpus allocation protocol](../terra-vm-zram-corpus-review-001/findings.md) to investigate fixed-order and allocator-history effects. Each guest used the same hash-verified experimental module and 26 corpus files, and each completed two module-load cycles, 312 per-corpus records, four-worker read/write validation, CPU1 hotplug, stock-device preservation and unload checks. The tested `ghy-simd` is the earlier guarded SIMD prototype, not the later userspace sampling candidate. Host stock ZRAM and CPU setting hashes matched before and after each guest; no experimental host module was loaded.

Corpus order was shuffled deterministically per guest. Seeds 471 and 472 also shuffled algorithm order, but both happened to put SIMD GHY last. Seed 482 was deliberately selected to put SIMD GHY first as a counterbalance; it is not an independently drawn random seed. Full orders, raw nine-field allocation observations, input hashes and original console logs are retained. `ranges.csv` covers all six sequential cycles and all algorithms, without dropping unfavorable cases. The two cycles within each guest are not independent trials.

For the sparse-word fixture, SIMD GHY used 274,432–290,816 allocated bytes across the six observations, while standard LZ4 used 520,192–532,480 and zstd 425,984–438,272. For Bash ELF, SIMD GHY used 679,936–716,800 bytes, LZ4 663,552–684,032 and zstd 593,920–614,400. On random data, all three used 520,192 bytes and stored all 127 pages raw/huge. On zero pages, all used zero allocated bytes due to same-filled handling. These are observed ranges of small 127-page fixtures, not confidence intervals or workload-weighted gains. Other fixture ranges and LZO-RLE/private/scalar controls are retained in the CSV and JSON.

Allocation varies even when compressed bytes are unchanged because class allocation and page history differ. Some small corpus totals exceed source payload due to allocation granularity/fragmentation; they must not be interpreted as a stable per-page cost at scale. These guest measurements establish neither native ZRAM improvement nor real browsing/compilation pressure behavior, CPU time, FPU overhead, package energy, battery life or responsiveness. Native experimental module testing remains gated on verified recovery.

The running Fedora kernel configuration has `CONFIG_ZSMALLOC_STAT` disabled. Consequently, the exact-kernel guest cannot expose the zsmalloc per-class debug statistics needed to determine how much of the observed difference comes from size-class occupancy versus other pool effects. In particular, the clustered fixture's much smaller GHY allocation despite zstd's smaller compressed byte total is an observation, not evidence of a specific mechanism. A separately built statistics-enabled diagnostic kernel could investigate that mechanism, but would not provide exact-stock-kernel performance evidence.
