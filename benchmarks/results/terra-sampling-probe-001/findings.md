# Untimed predictions for existing sampling gates

This diagnostic evaluates the existing 16- and 64-word zero-sampling rules and their 32-byte repeat shortcut without modifying codec selection. It aggregates predictions on a fresh owned synthetic SQLite process and on all 36 preserved synthetic/executable corpora. Source and input hashes are retained in the JSON. Each page is actually encoded and round-tripped by the parent and scalar LZ4 controls; the hypothetical framed LZ4 size is `min(4096, LZ4 bytes + 8)`. This is not an implementation benchmark and does not establish gate overhead, full selection behavior, kernel allocation, energy or ZRAM performance. Same-word pages are excluded as in earlier samples.

In the SQLite sample, the 16-word rule rejected 102 of 128 anonymous pages, including three of the 18 GZRAM-mode pages. Replacing just rejected pages with framed LZ4 would reduce their capped total from 170,929 to 167,351 bytes; the other 15 GZRAM-mode pages remained selected even though all had smaller LZ4 frames. The 64-word rule rejected 101 pages, including two of the 18 GZRAM-mode pages; it left 16 selected despite smaller LZ4 frames. Results are a small reservoir from a generated database, not a real reclaim distribution. Rejection might avoid failed GZRAM trial work on the many pages already in hybrid LZ4 mode, but that CPU effect is not measured here.

Both rules selected every page in the `sparse-words` fixture, preserving the parent's 84,588 bytes compared with 139,438 framed LZ4 bytes. Conversely, both rejected all pages of adversarial `sample64-sparse-miss`, where their hypothetical fallback would increase bytes from 51,712 to 66,800 (+29.2%). They also selected all pages in the adversarial dense-hit fixture where GZRAM trial work failed and output fell back. These fixtures were deliberately designed to challenge fixed sampling; they are not workload-frequency estimates. All 36 corpus results, including regressions, are in `corpus.json`.

The gates are useful hypotheses for avoiding rejected work, but these observations do not justify promotion or an assumed speedup. Any actual combined candidate needs matching frame-capacity semantics, independent correctness and sanitizer checks, randomized physical timing and package-energy controls, and broader sampled workloads. GRUB recovery remains unverified and no native experimental kernel test was run.
