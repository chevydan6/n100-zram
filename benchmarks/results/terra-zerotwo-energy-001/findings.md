Allocator-rounded ZRAM usage is not derived from bytes.

Absolute values are pooled medians; percentages are medians of the three session median paired-round ratios, and do not necessarily equal the ratios of pooled medians. Negative time changes mean less time, not the same percentage increase in throughput. Session ranges are descriptive, not confidence intervals. ## Decision and interpretation

Reject both zero-prefix selection rules as general-purpose policies. In the `zeroguard-random` adversarial sparse fixture, the one-match AVX2 candidate increased median compression time from 5,606 to 36,030 ns/page (paired +545.1%), package energy from 11.53 to 71.3 µJ/page (paired +560.8%), and output from 1,176.38 to 1,877.18 bytes/page (+59.6%) relative to the parent. Requiring two matches prevented that selection, but still added a paired 5.0% compression time in the same fixture, with identical output. On `zerotwoguard-random`, two repeated keys defeated the stricter rule: 5,620 to 36,839 ns/page (+552.9%), 11.41 to 76.63 µJ/page (+515.2%), and 1,198.16 to 1,904.78 bytes/page (+59.0%). The adversarial fixtures establish failure modes, not their frequency in a workload.

On the small separately sampled compiler workload, both policies improved five selected pages, as documented in [the candidate checkpoint](../terra-zero-prefix-guard-001/findings.md). That size-only evidence does not outweigh the demonstrated regressions or establish a representative ZRAM gain. In the current matched corpus, the stricter candidate versus 4 KiB-table LZ4 had 46.0% higher compression time on incompressible data and 36.9% higher time on period127; it had 69.5% lower time and 38.9% fewer bytes on sparse words, and 12.9% lower time and 5.0% fewer bytes on mixed pages. These are synthetic cases, not a workload-weighted overall result. Package-energy measurements include other package activity; session changes, especially on small batches, can vary substantially. No laptop battery-life inference is warranted.

The runs used the physical Intel N100, Linux 7.2.11-200.fc44.x86_64, compiler and build inputs in the manifests, on battery with `powersave` and the configured 700–800 MHz range. CPU0 sampled frequency was approximately 800 MHz; these results are not directly matched to previous dynamic 700–3400 MHz AC runs. The audit checked 11,880 timing rows and package-energy readings, all randomized task positions, original artifacts and unchanged ZRAM/settings. The CPU package measurements do not include kernel SIMD management or ZRAM I/O, and compressed frame bytes are not allocator-rounded storage.

Do not promote either guard. Preserve the parent and the negative results. The next credible investigation is a selection rule whose full decision and fallback cost is measured on broader observed anonymous-memory samples and adversarial pages; an extra match by itself is insufficient evidence. Native kernel testing still requires verified GRUB recovery and representative memory-pressure comparisons.
