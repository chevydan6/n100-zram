# Longer-lived browser pressure pilot

Six randomized matched pairs compared the running stock LZ4 and zstd ZRAM algorithms on the physical Intel N100 (`7.2.11-n100-gzram-btf`). The established separate 192 MiB high-priority temporary swap device was used; existing zstd and hibernation swaps stayed active. The disposable user-session cgroup retained `MemoryHigh=384M`, `MemoryMax=768M`, `MemorySwapMax=192M`, and `TasksMax=512`, but used a larger 128 MiB synthetic working set and two persistent generated local Chromium pages, each with a touched 16 MiB JavaScript array. Ten GCC compilations, 20 local DevTools DOM interactions and page-content checks ran over each approximately 24-second trial, including 20 seconds of intentional sleeps. All 12 trials passed. The temporary swap was deactivated and removed, and existing swap settings were verified unchanged. The separate 10 ms scheduler-lateness probe also ran; it is not an interactive GUI latency measurement.

The [table](tables.md) gives medians, observed ranges and median same-round LZ4/zstd percentage changes. LZ4 versus zstd had median workload time 24.078 versus 24.474 s (-1.5% paired), browser interaction 1.234 versus 1.210 ms (+3.0%), compilation 213.4 versus 225.5 ms (-6.2%) and package energy 58.64 versus 61.49 J (-4.3%). Median sampled peak temporary-device allocation was 86.8 versus 71.6 MiB (+21.4%); median peak cgroup swap was 191.0 versus 189.1 MiB (+0.8%), close to the 192 MiB cgroup swap limit. The per-trial p95 browser-interaction statistic used only 20 samples and varied widely; it is not a reliable tail-latency distribution. The previous pilot's apparent 10.7% median interaction advantage did not repeat here.

These are generated headless pages, not actual user browsing or proof of laptop-wide performance. Six pairs are limited, the high-priority temporary swap is global, and 0.5-second allocation sampling can miss peaks or include other processes. Package energy includes the intentional sleeps, monitoring and unrelated CPU activity; it is not whole-system energy or battery life. The near-limit cgroup may experience reclaim behavior specific to these artificial limits and does not model the entire laptop being nearly full. No complete CPU-utilization accounting, hardware cycles, GUI input latency or long-term stability was measured. Results show the memory/speed tradeoff and do not justify changing normal zstd settings. Raw randomized order, observations, source and a successful no-test-swap smoke run are retained.
