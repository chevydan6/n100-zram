# A shifted sampling confirmation: negative diagnostic

A proposed untimed selection rule would retain the existing 16-word gate and, only if it rejected a page, also inspect 16 words shifted by four bytes; either gate selecting the page would permit the existing parent GZRAM trial. This analysis predicts selection only. It does not implement the rule or measure runtime, SIMD state, CPU energy, ZRAM, or application outcomes. Page-size and framing assumptions match the earlier [sampling probe](../terra-sampling-probe-001/findings.md).

On the preserved `sample64-sparse-miss` fixture the second set detects zero words missed by both old gates, predicting preservation of the parent's 51,712 bytes rather than 66,800 framed LZ4 bytes. It also predicts selecting one additional page on the `mixed-pages` corpus and leaves all dense-hit pages selected. That isolated success is insufficient to justify a rule.

A new independently constructed adversarial fixture puts nonzero words at both sampling lattices and 64 random nonzero words at other positions. The proposed union rejects all 128 pages despite the parent yielding 68,096 bytes and framed LZ4 yielding 101,567 bytes (+49.2%). `adversarial.py` records its deterministic construction and SHA-256 without storing pages. Any fixed sampling positions can be adversarially unrepresentative; this fixture demonstrates a concrete failure, not expected workload frequency.

On a new generated, owned SQLite workload reservoir, the original 16-word rule rejected 99 of 128 anonymous pages; the shifted union rejected 84. All 11 GZRAM-mode pages in this small sample remained selected even though their LZ4 frames were smaller. The extra 15 selected pages were already LZ4 mode in the parent, so the shift could reintroduce failed GZRAM work, but this diagnostic did not time it. Sample content, CPU activity and reclaim selection are not representative of general workloads. `corpus.json` retains all 36 preserved inputs; `sqlite.json` contains aggregate outcomes and hashes.

Reject this confirmation rule as a general policy: it exchanges one aligned blind spot for another and reduces the opportunity to avoid rejected trials. The analysis motivates looking beyond fixed-position sampling, but no alternative should be promoted without measured complete-codec tradeoffs. Native testing remains gated on verified recovery.
