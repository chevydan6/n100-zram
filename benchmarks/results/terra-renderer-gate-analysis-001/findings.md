# Sampling-gate predictions on renderer aggregates

This analysis reuses the two [previously collected disposable renderer samples](../terra-owned-renderer-sample-001/findings.md). It does not access new processes or page data. It combines per-gate selected/rejected aggregate counts and capped logical bytes to predict a policy that leaves selected pages at their parent size and sends rejected pages to GHY-framed LZ4. These are predictions for the previously described gate, not actual candidate timings or a new validation of encoder equivalence. `analyze.py` checks totals and records hashes of its inputs; all four examined policies are in `result.json`.

For the 16-word gate in trial 006, 77 of 113 non-same-word anonymous pages remained selected; 60 selected pages had a smaller framed LZ4 alternative. Only three rejected pages had a smaller alternative. The predicted capped total is 188,790 bytes versus the parent's 191,498 (-1.41%); direct unframed LZ4 was 153,400 bytes. In trial 007, 77 of 126 remained selected; 50 had a smaller framed LZ4 alternative and two had a larger alternative. Three rejected pages had a smaller alternative. The predicted total is 197,719 versus 199,191 (-0.74%); direct LZ4 was 173,957 bytes. Framing makes the direct LZ4 column different from the framed fallback calculation.

The result helps explain why the gate's measured synthetic speedups do not establish improved renderer memory use: it is designed to skip some trials, not to identify most pages on which GZRAM expands relative to LZ4. Page selection remains subject to the original severe nonresident/readability bias; the generated single-tab renderer sample is not a reclaim distribution. No speed, energy, kernel, allocator, responsiveness or whole-system claim follows. The known adversarial sparse regressions remain material and no sampling policy is promoted.
