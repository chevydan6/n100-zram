# Where the compiler sample loses compression ratio

This is a retrospective aggregation of already published samples 005–007, not a new measurement. Three owned compiler-child samples used different seeds and lifetime reservoirs, each sampling 64 pages per class per compilation. They can contain repeated pages within or across snapshots and are not an independent, population-weighted sample of reclaimed pages. No raw page contents were retained. `analyze.py` regenerates `result.json` from the previously committed aggregates.

Among non-same-word anonymous pages that the parent hybrid encoded in GZRAM mode, GZRAM frames were larger than the direct scalar LZ4 control on 81 of 84 pages in sample 005, 75 of 78 in 006, and 76 of 80 in 007. The corresponding aggregate GZRAM versus LZ4 logical payloads were 123,032 versus 71,109 bytes; 115,228 versus 71,601; and 95,504 versus 58,930. In the hybrid's LZ4 mode, the frame was eight bytes larger per page in these samples, consistent with GHY1 framing. Values are capped logical payloads and do not model kernel huge-page cutoff or allocator rounding.

This evidence supports investigating selection before committing to GZRAM on these compiler pages, while preserving its demonstrated advantages on sparse-word and mixed synthetic pages. It does not establish a general traffic distribution, application responsiveness or energy outcome. A useful next experiment must characterize independently collected page populations from additional workloads and assess classification and rejected-work costs, not fit another rule to these three small snapshots. Measuring both compressors before choosing the smaller would guarantee a payload-size choice only under matching capacity and framing rules, but incurs both encoding costs and scratch-space requirements; the recent matched adversarial timing already shows why treating size alone as success is insufficient. Native kernel or ZRAM use remains gated on recovery validation.
