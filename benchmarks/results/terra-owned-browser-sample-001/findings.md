# Disposable Chromium browser-process page samples

Two separate headless Chromium processes were started with fresh temporary profiles and a generated local single-tab document. Background networking, sync, extensions and component updates were disabled and DNS resolution was directed to fail. The process ran at nice 10 under the exclusive native-test lock. The sampler checked that the sampled PID was its own direct Chromium child and read only present, readable pages; it retained aggregate counts and source hashes, no page contents or addresses. The browser process and temporary profile were removed after each run. Existing browser profiles and unrelated processes were not sampled. The local page was supplied on the command line, but successful page rendering was not independently verified.

This is **only the browser process**, not Chromium renderers, GPU processes, browser multitasking, users' browsing data, pages selected for reclaim, or a representative browser workload. File-executable pages can be dropped rather than swapped. Each trial lasted about 25 seconds, with at most 128 selected pages per mapping class. Large mapped regions made 2,815 and 2,844 sampling attempts fail or find nonresident pages; selection is therefore highly biased toward eligible readable pages. The samples can overlap in content across runs and are not population-weighted. `environment.json` gives the CPU, kernel, browser version and binary hash captured after the runs; JSON records source and codec hashes.

Among non-same-word anonymous pages, trial 1 had 114 pages: parent AVX2 GHY capped logical payload 178,077 bytes versus 137,308 for direct 4 KiB scalar LZ4 (+29.7%). Of 49 pages encoded in GZRAM mode, all 49 had larger GHY payloads than the paired LZ4 result. Trial 2 had 123 non-same-word pages: 196,788 versus 154,702 bytes (+27.2%); 55 of 56 GZRAM-mode pages were larger and one was smaller. LZ4-mode GHY frames include eight-byte container overhead. Each sampled page round-tripped through the existing userspace codecs. Capping sizes at 4 KiB does not model the actual ZRAM huge-object threshold or allocator rounding.

The results reinforce the previously observed selection weakness in another application process, but they cannot establish browser or ZRAM performance, memory savings, energy, responsiveness, or a general traffic distribution. The large excluded fraction and missing renderer are material limitations. Stock zstd ZRAM and existing swap were not altered; experimental native kernel testing remains gated on recovery.
