# Zstd level 2 physical pressure follow-up

After the [direct-device level screen](../terra-zstd-level-screen-001/findings.md), the physical N100 ran randomized matched trials of default zstd, zstd level 2 and LZ4 using separate 192 MiB high-priority temporary ZRAM swap, while the existing stock zstd and hibernation swap stayed active. The workload used the same bounded cgroup, 128 MiB synthetic working set, two persistent generated headless browser pages, ten GCC compilations and 20 local DevTools DOM interactions per trial as the preceding sustained pilot. It included 20 seconds of intentional sleeps per workload. Level 2 was set on the temporary device via `algorithm_params`; the active stock device was not reconfigured.

The first run stopped in round 4 while removing a disposable Chromium profile because the directory was still being populated by a child process. Four complete matched rounds and one extra zstd-2 trial were retained, as were the failure and samples; temporary swap removal and preservation of the original swap settings succeeded. A second independently seeded run used process-group termination plus a short cleanup delay and completed two further full rounds. The source and raw results for both runs are retained. The cleanup change adds fixed overhead to the latter two rounds; paired percentage comparisons are within each round. The first run's incomplete round is excluded from the matched analysis.

The [table](tables.md) shows medians over the six complete sets and the median of same-round percentage changes. Zstd level 2 versus default had workload 24.516 versus 24.532 s (-0.1% paired), browser interaction 1.226 versus 1.196 ms (+4.1%), compilation 222.6 versus 224.5 ms, package energy 62.82 versus 67.01 J (-4.6%), and sampled peak device allocation 72.4 versus 71.4 MiB. LZ4 is included as an additional control. Pooling absolute medians across runs with different cleanup overhead is descriptive; within-round changes are the more meaningful comparison. The results do not demonstrate an end-to-end speed improvement or sufficient evidence to change the default. Six short paired rounds and overlapping variability do not establish the energy difference or rule out benefits elsewhere.

The 0.5-second direct-device screen found faster compression but larger output or slower reads on some corpora; this pressure pilot uses synthetic pages and headless generated browsing, not representative user browsing. Cgroup swap approached its 192 MiB limit, allocation sampling every 0.5 seconds can miss peaks, and globally prioritized temporary swap can include other processes. Package energy includes intentional sleeps, cleanup, monitoring and background CPU activity, not battery life. Hardware cycles, complete CPU accounting, real GUI input latency and long-term stability were not measured. Stock configuration remains unchanged and no routine-use promotion follows.
