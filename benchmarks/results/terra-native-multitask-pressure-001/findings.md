# Physical concurrent browser and compile pressure pilot

Six randomized rounds compared GHY bitdirect, LZ4, zstd and lzo-rle on the physical Intel N100 (`7.2.11-n100-gzram-btf`), for 24 trials. Each ran a disposable user-session cgroup with `MemoryHigh=384M`, `MemoryMax=768M`, `MemorySwapMax=192M` and `TasksMax=512`, a 128 MiB synthetic working set, four compilation syntax checks, and eight headless Chromium launches in pairs concurrent with compilation and memory reads. Each browser loaded a generated local page using a disposable profile and had its completion marker checked. The temporary 192 MiB high-priority ZRAM swap device was deactivated and reset after each trial and removed at the end. Existing zstd ZRAM and hibernation swap remained active and their configuration matched afterward. All workload checks passed. A separate controller thread sampled lateness against 10 ms deadlines; this is a scheduler probe, not an interactive application response measurement.

The [table](tables.md) contains absolute medians and same-round paired changes. GHY's median workload time was 7.605 s versus LZ4's 7.566 s (paired median +1.4%); median browser launch was 1.822 versus 1.804 s (+1.5%); and package energy was 48.62 versus 48.12 J (+0.3%). Against zstd, the respective paired median changes were -6.5%, -6.8% and -6.9%; zstd's median sampled peak device allocation was 63.7 MiB versus GHY's 71.7 MiB. LZO-RLE is also shown. This workload does not demonstrate the 10% real-world target and does not establish a definitive winner: only six short trials per codec were collected.

Median sampled peak cgroup swap ranged 146.0–156.5 MiB, confirming meaningful swap activity; 0.5-second sampling can miss peaks. The higher-priority test swap is global, so other processes may contribute to device allocation and compression work. CPU-package energy includes other work, service setup and monitoring; it is not full-system energy or battery life. The p95 probe reports controller-thread scheduling lateness, not GUI or browser interaction tail latency. Raw cgroup CPU samples, faults, operation times, energy, allocation and scheduling samples are retained, but complete CPU-utilization accounting, hardware cycles, whole-laptop energy and long-duration stability were not measured. Generated local headless pages do not model actual user multitasking; frequent fresh browser profiles and repeated corpus pages limit representativeness. Results cannot justify production promotion. The user accepted manual reset if the laptop hung; independent automatic hard-hang recovery remains unverified.
