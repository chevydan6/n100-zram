# Disposable Chromium renderer samples

Two headless Chromium instances were launched in separate, dedicated systemd user services with temporary profiles and a generated local document. The sampler considered only processes in its own dedicated service cgroup with both a renderer-type argument and that run's unique profile path; it rechecked the PID's parent and executable name before reading present pages. It retained counts and hashes, not page bytes, addresses, user profile data or unrelated process data. No browser sandbox settings were weakened. Temporary browser processes and profiles were removed after each run. Renderer type/profile are verified, but the specific renderer hosting the generated document was not identified and successful rendering was not independently verified.

Trial 006 retained 113 non-same-word anonymous pages: the AVX2 parent produced 191,498 capped logical bytes versus 153,400 for 4 KiB scalar LZ4 (+24.8%). All 63 pages encoded in GZRAM mode were larger than their same-page LZ4 controls. Trial 007 retained 126 such pages: 199,191 versus 173,957 bytes (+14.5%); 53 of 55 GZRAM-mode pages were larger and two smaller. The remainder includes LZ4-mode eight-byte GHY framing and raw pages. Each retained page round-tripped through existing userspace codecs. Bytes are capped logical payloads, not actual ZRAM allocator usage.

These are small, unweighted samples from a generated single-tab test, not users' browser sessions or kernel-selected swap traffic. The sampler found 116 and 137 eligible anonymous reads and retained at most 128 per class. It also counted 6,707 and 6,664 failed or nonresident attempts across mapping classes, causing substantial selection bias. Renderer-candidate counts count repeated discovery passes, not distinct renderers. Pages and processes can overlap in content; CPU time, energy, browser responsiveness and real memory pressure were not measured. The browser binary environment record was captured after the preceding browser-process trials, not rehashed during these two renderer trials.

Attempts 001–005 safely found no eligible renderer and are retained with their sampler versions and failure records. Earlier discovery used leader-thread ancestry and exact null-separated argument matching; Chromium's rewritten command lines were not matched. The successful discovery requires a dedicated test-service cgroup and unique profile. The trial 006 source snapshot says “descendants”; its actual check is cgroup plus profile, as corrected in the trial 007 source. Stock zstd ZRAM was not changed. This evidence does not demonstrate a native ZRAM improvement or resolve boot recovery.
