Allocator-rounded ZRAM usage is not derived from bytes.

Absolute values are pooled medians; percentages are medians of the three session median paired-round ratios, and do not necessarily equal the ratios of pooled medians. Negative time changes mean less time, not the same percentage increase in throughput. Session ranges are descriptive, not confidence intervals. Package energy, when available, includes background package activity and harness bookkeeping; it is not whole-laptop energy or battery life. ## Interpretation and decision

The 16-word AVX2 gate improved userspace compression time versus its same-run GHY parent on random input (6,055.7 to 5,212.3 ns/page, paired -14.3%), period127 (4,099.6 to 3,137.5, -23.9%), bash executable pages (40,679.1 to 39,216.2, -3.6%) and mixed pages (14,015.8 to 13,571.0, -3.5%); compressed frame sizes were unchanged in those fixtures. Paired package-energy changes were -14.2%, -28.5%, -3.6% and -2.3%, respectively, although package readings include background activity and their session variability is retained in `paired.json`. On sparse words the gate was 1.4% slower with unchanged size. The 64-word gate shows similar but generally smaller compression-time savings and higher sampling cost on these fixtures.

The adversarial sample64-sparse-miss fixture is a substantial regression: the 16-word candidate increased compression time from 2,259.3 to 5,972.2 ns/page (paired +163.1%), package energy from 5.3 to 14.4 µJ/page (+176.8%) and frame bytes from 404.0 to 521.9 (+29.2%) versus the parent. The 64-word candidate also lost on that fixture. Dense-hit pages demonstrate that the sampled positions can admit expensive failed GZRAM work. These constructed cases are not estimates of workload frequency, but they demonstrate a real weakness in the selection method.

Against 4 KiB-table scalar LZ4, the 16-word candidate's compression time was 21.8% higher on random and 4.1% higher on period127, but 69.8% lower on sparse words and 15.7% lower on mixed pages. Bytes were 38.9% and 5.0% lower on sparse and mixed pages respectively, while period127 was 5.2% larger. These are userspace fixture results; they are not an overall compressor ranking, real ZRAM allocated memory, kernel SIMD overhead, application latency, or battery life. The physical N100 was on battery with `powersave` and a 700–800 MHz configured range; compare the earlier dynamic-frequency AC runs only with appropriate caution. The audit passed 11,340 timing rows, all with package energy available, checked randomized task positions and preserved artifacts, and verified the observed stock ZRAM and frequency settings were unchanged.

Keep both sampling combinations as prototypes and do not promote them as general defaults. The measurable fast-path gain justifies examining safer selection, but the adversarial time, energy and size losses and the small, unweighted SQLite sample do not demonstrate the 10% real-world acceptance target. The next step is to test a policy that avoids committing solely from fixed sample positions and measure its complete overhead on independently collected workloads; preserve the existing regressions as controls. Native experimental ZRAM remains gated on verified boot recovery.
