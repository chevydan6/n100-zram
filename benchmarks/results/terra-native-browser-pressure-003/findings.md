# Randomized physical browser-and-swap pressure test

On the physical Lenovo N100 running `7.2.11-n100-gzram-btf`, eight rounds randomized zstd, LZ4, lzo-rle and GHY bitdirect in each round (seed 52735; 32 trials). Each used a temporary 128 MiB high-priority ZRAM swap device, while the pre-existing zstd ZRAM and hibernation swap stayed active. A disposable user-session cgroup (`MemoryHigh=256M`, `MemoryMax=640M`, `MemorySwapMax=128M`, `TasksMax=512`) ran a 128 MiB synthetic working set, two GCC syntax checks and two generated local headless Chromium page launches. Page marker and sampled data checks passed in every trial. User browsing data and profiles were not used. The device was swapped off and reset between trials, then removed; original swap configuration was verified unchanged. Battery was 44% and discharging at the final check.

The [table](tables.md) gives absolute medians and medians of same-round percentage changes. GHY's median workload was 2.825 s and browser launch 1.251 s, compared with LZ4's 2.634 s and 1.125 s; paired median changes were +2.9% and +4.2% respectively. GHY's median package total was 19.88 J versus LZ4's 20.19 J, while the median of the eight same-round energy ratios was +7.8%; these are different statistics and should not be calculated from the two pooled medians. Against stock zstd, the paired median changes for GHY were -6.9% workload time, -6.7% browser-launch time and -9.6% package energy. These short runs do not establish a repeatable 10% real-world improvement. LZO-RLE results are also in the table.

Median sampled peak cgroup swap was 71.4 MiB for GHY and 69.6 MiB for LZ4. Sampled peak temporary-device allocation was 36.6 and 36.3 MiB, respectively; sample frequency was 0.5 s and may miss peaks. The temporary swap is globally higher-priority, so other processes can use it; allocation is not guaranteed to be workload-only. CPU-package energy covers service setup, monitoring and other package activity and is not whole-laptop energy or battery life. Cgroup CPU samples and major-fault counts are retained but no complete CPU-utilization, cycles, foreground desktop responsiveness, tail-latency confidence or whole-system energy measurement was made. Eight short runs and generated headless pages cannot represent real browser multitasking or prove long-term stability. Raw randomized order, samples and results are preserved. Further tests need realistic application traffic, independent foreground latency, longer energy windows, and a means to quantify global-swap interference before any production decision.
