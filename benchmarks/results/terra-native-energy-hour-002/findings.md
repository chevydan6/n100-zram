# Sustained physical ZRAM package-energy experiment

Two independently shuffled runs on the physical Intel N100, each with nine rounds of eight corpora and six codecs, completed 864 trials over roughly 58 minutes of active measurement. Each trial used a temporary non-swap ZRAM device, a warmup write, a two-second repeated direct-I/O write batch and a two-second repeated read batch. The final read was verified. Separate elapsed time, calling-thread CPU time, CPU-package RAPL energy, operation counts and allocator statistics were recorded. Every trial passed; the temporary device was removed after each run, and active zstd swap configuration was preserved. The battery went from 78% to 68% to 58% across the two experiments; those whole-experiment readings do not identify the energy of any codec.

[Complete tables](tables.md) report 18-sample medians and the median of same-round candidate/LZ4 changes. Energy is normalized per MiB of completed I/O, time per page, and paired observations occur within the same corpus block in randomized codec order. For sparse words, GHY bitdirect used a median 2.767 mJ/MiB writing and 1.736 mJ/MiB reading versus LZ4's 7.025 and 3.099; paired median changes were -60.7% and -44.2%, with 286,720 versus 524,288 allocated bytes. For period127, bitdirect write energy was 2.951 versus 2.136 mJ/MiB (+38.0% paired) and allocated bytes were equal. For bash executable pages, bitdirect used 16.192 versus 15.316 mJ/MiB to write (+5.7% paired) and allocated 700,416 versus 667,648 bytes. For random pages, bitdirect used 4.004 versus 3.065 mJ/MiB to write (+30.2% paired), with equal allocation. Results vary by corpus; they do not support a universal GZRAM win.

RAPL measures CPU-package energy including background activity, not wall power or battery life; it is not an isolated codec energy meter. Longer two-second intervals improve counter resolution compared with the prior millisecond pilot, but repeated identical pages, write/read batching, active CPU frequency and thermal behavior, small allocation pools, Python/system-call overhead and lack of actual memory pressure limit generalization. RAPL and timing differences may also include scheduling and other package activity. The runs did not measure application responsiveness, hardware cycles, full-system energy, representative swap activity or long-term stability; they cannot satisfy the 10% real-world acceptance target. Codec path counts were not recorded. Raw input hashes, random order, timings, energy and per-trial records are preserved. Earlier 800 MHz measurements are not directly comparable.
