# Generated-browser pressure harness attempts

The earlier synthetic cgroup pressure pilot completed, but this generated-browser extension did not yield a valid matched comparison. A disposable headless Chromium profile and local generated document were used; user browser data was not accessed. The first trial had `MemoryHigh=320M`, a 192 MiB synthetic working set and a 256 MiB temporary swap device. Chromium did not finish within 45 seconds; the temporary swap device was subsequently deactivated and removed, and the original active swaps and zstd setting matched before and after. The failure log and result are retained. The exact pressure cause is not established; the device was nearly full during the attempt, and other system swap activity could also have used it.

A second attempt reduced the working set to 128 MiB and limited temporary swap to 128 MiB with `MemoryHigh=384M`. LZ4 was the first randomized codec; Chromium again timed out after 25 seconds. Cgroup samples showed no swap activity in that failed trial, so memory pressure alone does not explain this timeout. Cleanup and stock swap preservation succeeded. A change to redirect Chromium output to files allowed the same workload to complete in a transient **user-session** cgroup without a temporary swap device, but a third system-service attempt again timed out on LZ4 without measured cgroup swapping; cleanup succeeded. A subsequent non-swap system-service smoke attempt failed with a D-Bus remote-peer disconnect. The boot journal also contains Chromium core-dump messages; their exact relationship to these failed launches has not been established. Selected errors and both retained attempts are provided. No codec comparison or real-world improvement can be inferred.

The system and user service environments differ, and the failed harness does not yet establish why Chromium fails in the system service. Do not treat the successful user-session smoke as validation of the privileged swap-test harness. Further work should isolate environment and browser process lifetime in a no-swap test, verify reliable output and renderer completion, then reintroduce bounded pressure. Repeatedly rerunning the failed pressure test without a diagnosis would not produce meaningful evidence. Physical recovery, global swap interference, package-energy noise and application-latency limitations from the previous pilot still apply.
