# Page-only zstd match-search skip candidate

Following the [instrumented profile](../terra-zstd-profile-001/findings.md), this opt-in x86-64 prototype changes the no-dictionary double-fast match finder's skip increment from 256 to 128 bytes only for blocks at most 4 KiB; larger blocks and dictionary paths retain the upstream behavior. Earlier skipping reduces match-search work but can change compressed frames and size. The patch uses standard zstd framing, not a new format. It is not an AVX2 optimization, and the value 128 is an experimental choice, not a measured universal N100 crossover. The source, build logs and results are retained.

Fresh control and candidate userspace Zstandard 1.5.7 libraries used identical GCC `-O3 -fPIC -march=x86-64-v3 -fno-tree-vectorize` flags apart from the candidate macro. Nine randomized same-round comparisons covered all 26 retained corpora, 128 pages each and 1,024 calls per timed batch. The [table](tables.md) reports paired time changes and all compressed-size regressions. Among 3,328 pages, 2,869 produced byte-identical frames; both frame variants decoded with candidate, control and independently loaded system libzstd. Truncated frames and undersized outputs returned errors. Full candidate ASan/UBSan validation passed 3,328 pages across four threads, including round trips, truncated input, buffer canaries and three mutations per frame. This is finite coverage, not a complete fuzz or stability proof.

Compared with the matched control, median paired compression time was -8.8% on random and -20.5% on sample16-dense-hit, but -0.2% on bash executable, -1.0% on mixed pages, +0.8% on sparse words and +3.0% on halfzero. The word-tail64 corpus grew from 272,896 to 289,280 bytes across 128 pages (+6.0%), and halfzero grew 0.145%; clusters50 compressed slightly smaller. These synthetic observations include Python/ctypes overhead, dynamic frequency and small samples; decompression differences are not intentionally targeted and may reflect library layout or noise. No package energy, hardware cycles, allocator-rounded ZRAM usage or application behavior was measured. The significant size regression and lack of broad whole-codec gain mean this candidate is not promoted to kernel integration or routine use. A [milder 192-byte candidate](../terra-zstd-n100-skip192-001/findings.md) is retained separately.
