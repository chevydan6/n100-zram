# Instrumented zstd profile on N100 page corpora

Upstream Zstandard 1.5.7 tag commit `f8745da6ff1ad1e7bab384bd1f9d742439278e99` was built as a static library with GCC `-O3 -pg -fno-omit-frame-pointer -march=x86-64-v3 -fno-tree-vectorize`. A small C harness compressed each of six retained 128-page corpora 1,000 times at level 3 on physical CPU 0, with a reusable compression context. Every corpus completed 128,000 pages. gprof reports and compressed raw gmon records are retained. This measures userspace compression; it is not the exact kernel build, ZRAM, energy or application performance. Profiling instrumentation, inlining and 10 ms sampling resolution distort percentages and hide costs attributed to inlined code; short-corpus estimates are coarse.

`ZSTD_compressBlock_doubleFast` accounted for approximately 60% of sampled self time on bash executable pages, 53% on libc pages, 56% on mixed pages, 49% on sparse words and 64% on random pages in these instrumented runs. This is evidence to examine the match finder, not proof of an exact fraction in native ZRAM. A page-only skip-policy candidate and a milder variant were evaluated separately; their speed and compressed-size tradeoffs must be considered in full, not inferred from this profile.
