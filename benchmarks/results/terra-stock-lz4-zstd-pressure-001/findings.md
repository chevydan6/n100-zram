# Physical stock LZ4 versus zstd swap-pressure comparison

Twelve randomized matched rounds compared the running kernel's stock `lz4` and `zstd` ZRAM algorithms on a separate temporary 192 MiB high-priority ZRAM swap device on the physical Intel N100 (`7.2.11-n100-gzram-btf`). The existing stock zstd ZRAM and hibernation swap stayed active throughout. Each disposable user-session cgroup used `MemoryHigh=384M`, `MemoryMax=768M`, `MemorySwapMax=192M`, and `TasksMax=512`, with a 128 MiB synthetic working set, eight real GCC `-O2 -c` compilations and 16 generated local headless Chromium page launches in concurrent pairs. Working-set samples and browser completion markers were checked. The test profile and compile outputs were temporary. All 24 trials passed. The device was swapped off and reset between trials and removed afterward; active-swap configuration was verified unchanged. Random order, source, progress and raw samples are retained. The battery was 40% at preflight and 38% at completion, discharging.

The [table](tables.md) reports pooled medians, ranges and medians of same-round LZ4/zstd percentage changes. LZ4 median workload time was 15.306 versus 15.985 s (-3.6% paired); browser launch median was 1.853 versus 1.944 s (-4.7%); package energy was 95.47 versus 99.65 J (-3.6%). Median compilation latency was 0.253 versus 0.256 s (+0.2% paired). LZ4's median sampled peak device allocation was 73.1 versus zstd's 63.6 MiB (+15.3% paired); median peak cgroup swap use was 163.6 versus 163.1 MiB (+0.8%). A separate controller-thread 10 ms deadline probe had median per-trial p95 lateness 0.705 versus 0.729 ms (+10.5% paired); this is not GUI response latency. Pooled absolute medians and median paired changes are distinct statistics. These observations suggest a modest LZ4 time/energy benefit in this workload at a memory cost, not a 10% overall improvement or proof of a safe configuration change.

The local generated headless pages and repeated compilation are controlled application exercises, not actual user browsing or a complete representative workload. Each trial lasted around 15 seconds; 12 pairs are still limited, and ranges show variability. The temporary swap's higher priority is global, so unrelated system pages may be routed to it and device allocation cannot be attributed solely to the cgroup. `mm_stat` was sampled at 0.5-second intervals and can miss peaks. Package RAPL includes service setup, monitoring and background CPU activity; it is not whole-system energy or battery life. The scheduler probe is not an interactive foreground-app tail-latency measurement. Raw cgroup CPU/fault samples are present, but complete CPU-utilization accounting, hardware cycles and long-term stability are unmeasured. There is no basis to promote a codec or alter normal zstd settings from this pilot alone.
