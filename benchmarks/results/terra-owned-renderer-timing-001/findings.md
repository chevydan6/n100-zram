# Disposable renderer pages: userspace codec timing pilot

This pilot samples present pages from renderers in a dedicated disposable headless Chromium service with a temporary profile and generated local document, then terminates the browser and compares codecs on the same retained anonymous pages in memory. No page contents or addresses are written. Renderer discovery and bias limitations match the [size-only renderer study](../terra-owned-renderer-sample-001/findings.md); the particular renderer for the document was not verified. Each process runs at nice 10 under the existing exclusive lock. Source and library hashes and aggregate results are retained in the JSON files. Stock ZRAM is not modified.

For each non-same-word page, a random permutation of six operations compares direct scalar LZ4 (4 KiB table), the AVX2 GHY parent, and the combined 16-word sampling candidate. Each operation uses three warmups followed by 32 calls. Decode uses a common parent AVX2 decoder entry for both GHY variants; LZ4 uses its matching decoder. Frames are validated against the source before timing, and the corrected harness verifies the final timed encoding against the initial frame and checks timed decoding. Wall and thread CPU clocks include Python/ctypes and loop overhead and warm the same page repeatedly. These numbers are not direct kernel codec timings, application throughput, cold-page behavior, or swap latency. CPU cycles, package energy and whole-system energy were **not measured**. Frequency and power telemetry were not recorded during the trials, so cross-run effects cannot be excluded.

The initial three trials 001–003 are retained. They recorded candidate and parent paired against LZ4, but did not report candidate-versus-parent per-page ratios or check final encoded-byte identity; their original harness is retained as `sampler-v1.py`. Trials 004–006 use the corrected harness and fresh browser processes; no previous data were overwritten. Their non-same-word anonymous samples contained 123, 122 and 126 pages, with 6,628, 6,631 and 6,677 failed/nonresident mapping attempts across classes respectively. These failures substantially bias the sample. Samples can overlap in content and are not kernel reclaim distributions.

In corrected trials 004–006, the sampling candidate's median paired compression wall-time changes relative to the parent were -2.08%, -1.19% and -0.99%; paired thread-CPU changes were -1.67%, -0.20% and -0.58%. These medians summarize per-page ratios and are not ratios of pooled absolute medians. For context, trial 004's absolute compression wall medians were 21,629 ns/page for LZ4, 18,428 for the parent and 18,449 for the candidate; the per-page median ratio can disagree in sign with ratios of these separate medians because page costs vary. Trials 005 and 006 absolute medians and all per-operation metrics are retained in the raw JSON. Small decoder differences should not be credited to codec changes, especially where inputs are identical; see the [shared-decoder identity analysis](../terra-shared-decoder-identity-001/findings.md).

Total encoded bytes (not allocator-rounded and not capped) in trials 004–006 were: LZ4 171,563 / 186,568 / 175,896; parent GHY 206,156 / 223,783 / 200,519; sampling GHY 203,642 / 222,573 / 200,519. GHY includes its framing while direct LZ4 does not. The candidate is smaller than its parent in two samples but remains larger than LZ4 in all three. These small, generated, highly selected samples and short warm batches do not establish real-world acceptance or energy benefit; the sampling gate's known adversarial regressions remain. No candidate is promoted, and native experimental testing remains gated on verified recovery.
