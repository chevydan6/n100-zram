# Generated-browser physical swap-pressure retry

The prior failures were investigated using user-session smoke tests and retained diagnostics. The first retry in this directory timed out under lzo-rle; a diagnostic retry using zstd also timed out, and Chromium explicitly reported `pthread_create` failures and inability to fork a renderer. The harness had set `TasksMax=64`, counting threads as tasks. Raising this test-only limit to 512 allowed all subsequent trials to complete. This identifies a concrete harness issue; it does not establish that every earlier timeout had the same cause. Raw failed trials and errors are retained.

The completed physical N100 pilot used a disposable headless Chromium profile and a local generated DOM page, a 128 MiB synthetic memory working set and two GCC syntax checks per trial. A user-session transient cgroup had `MemoryHigh=256M`, `MemoryMax=640M`, `MemorySwapMax=128M` and `TasksMax=512`. A separate temporary 128 MiB high-priority ZRAM swap device was used; stock zstd ZRAM and the hibernation swap stayed active. In each of two rounds zstd ran first as a diagnostic control and LZ4, lzo-rle and GHY bitdirect followed in randomized order. Thus this is not a fully randomized comparison, and two trials per codec are far too few for a reliable ranking. Global priority may direct unrelated system swap activity into the temporary device. Browser output was checked for a completion marker and working-set samples were verified. The temporary device was swapped off and removed, and the original swap configuration was verified afterward.

Observed peak sampled cgroup swap ranged 75,599,872–102,432,768 bytes. The two GHY trials reported browser launch medians of 1.15 and 1.13 seconds and overall workload times 2.62 and 2.59 seconds; LZ4 reported 1.35 and 1.17 seconds and 3.08 and 2.68 seconds; zstd reported 1.60 and 1.21 seconds and 3.58 and 2.75 seconds. Package-energy totals measured around service execution, including monitoring and background CPU activity, were 19.94/20.31 J for GHY, 21.76/19.24 J for LZ4 and 32.00/20.59 J for zstd. These are raw exploratory observations, not evidence of repeatable energy or responsiveness gains. Workloads are generated and headless, not user browsing; measured total package energy is not battery life. Sampling was every 0.5 seconds and may miss allocation peaks. No general 10% real-world acceptance, hardware cycles or long-duration stability claim follows. Raw samples and results are retained for all four codecs, including lzo-rle. Next use more fully randomized matched trials, verify measured cgroup swap allocation and foreground responsiveness, and extend to realistic browser multitasking before a go/no-go decision.
